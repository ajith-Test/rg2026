UNDERSTAND ATS

What is ATS and why does it matter?

ATS stands for Applicant Tracking System. Employers use ATS software to collect, organize, search and sometimes rank applications before recruiters review them. An ATS can extract details such as job titles, skills, education, dates and other resume content, then compare the application with the requirements of a role.

01

It parses your resume

The system attempts to turn your resume into structured information that recruiters can search and filter.

02

It looks for relevance

Relevant skills, qualifications, job titles and terminology from the job description can affect how well your application matches.

03

Formatting can matter

Complex columns, tables, graphics, text boxes and unusual layouts can sometimes make information harder for a parser to interpret.

ATS-friendly does not mean “stuff your resume with keywords.” The goal is accurate parsing, clear structure and relevant content. Your resume still needs to make sense to a human recruiter and should always use truthful, job-relevant information.
1

Apply

You submit your resume to an employer or job platform.

2

Parse

ATS software extracts information from the document.

3

Match

Relevant information may be compared with the role requirements.

4

Review

Recruiters review applications that meet their process and criteria.

Why be careful with ad-heavy “free” sites?

“Free” does not automatically mean unsafe, and advertisements do not automatically mean a website steals your data. However, ad-heavy sites can contain distracting banners, pop-ups, third-party redirects or misleading buttons. Clicking an unexpected ad or download link can take you away from the resume tool and may expose you to third-party tracking or unsafe pages.

Your resume can contain personal information such as your phone number, email, employment history and education. Always check where you are clicking and who is receiving your information.
